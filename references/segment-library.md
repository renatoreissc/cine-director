# Segment Library — cine-director

Catalog of segment → visual conventions (shots, camera, lighting, pacing, SFX),
plus a universal method for deriving conventions for any segment not listed. **A
segment never dictates every choice on its own** — always adapt to the specific
brief (the product, the person, the mood the user actually described). The
"grounding" step (SKILL.md Step 2, WebSearch) exists to confirm these defaults still
match 2-3 real leading brands in that exact segment today, not to skip research.

---

## Universal beat taxonomy (use to derive ANY segment)

Every strong ~20s branded film is some ordering of these six beats. Pick the
instances that fit the actual brief, then sequence and pace them.

1. **Hook / cold open** — a kinetic or striking opener that earns the next 3 seconds
   (a detail in motion, a dramatic reveal-in-progress, an unexpected angle).
2. **Establish** — where are we, who/what is the subject; a wide or environmental
   shot that grounds the scene.
3. **Introduce** — the person/product/vehicle in a clean, flattering framing that
   locks identity before the camera gets adventurous.
4. **Core action / demonstration** — the subject doing the thing the brief is about
   (driving, wearing, using, cooking, building, presenting).
5. **Hero detail** — a macro or tight insert on the texture/material/expression
   that sells the premium feel.
6. **Climax / reveal + closing brand frame** — the payoff shot, often the most
   controlled and static of the film, sometimes holding on a clean brand moment.

**Pacing instinct:** slow down (camera settles, longer hold) on Introduce and the
closing frame; speed up (quicker cuts, more movement) on the Hook and Core action.

## Cataloged segments

### Automotive
- **Shots:** low-angle tracking alongside the vehicle, slow orbit around the body,
  drone flyover/reveal on open road or dramatic terrain, macro on badge/wheel/light
  details, interior POV from the driver's seat.
- **Camera:** wide 24mm for road/scale shots, macro 100mm for details, smooth
  gimbal/drone moves — never handheld (handheld reads cheap for this segment).
- **Lighting:** high-contrast chiaroscuro with hard specular hits on paint and
  chrome; golden hour or moody overcast for exteriors; practical ambient glow for
  interior/night shots.
- **Drone:** yes — exterior reveals, road/terrain flyovers.
- **SFX:** engine note, tire grip, door thud, wind, gravel/asphalt texture.
- **Variations:** performance/sports car → aggressive low angles, speed ramps,
  tighter framing. Family/utility vehicle → warmer light, more human moments
  (loading, family), softer pacing. Electric → quieter SFX palette, clean minimal
  environments, emphasis on silence/smoothness rather than engine sound.

### Fashion / apparel
- **Shots:** editorial stillness broken by a single deliberate camera move per
  beat, negative space around the figure, macro on fabric/texture, a walk or turn
  that shows movement of the garment.
- **Camera:** 50-85mm for the figure, slow dolly or a single push-in per beat, rare
  handheld only for a deliberately raw/street aesthetic.
- **Lighting:** soft directional key, clean high-key studio look OR moody
  environmental light depending on the brand tier (luxury = controlled studio;
  streetwear = available/harder light).
- **Drone:** rarely — only for a location/lifestyle shoot with a strong landscape
  element.
- **SFX:** fabric movement, footsteps, ambient room tone; minimal.
- **Variations:** luxury/couture → slower pacing, more negative space, fewer cuts.
  Streetwear/youth → faster cuts, handheld energy, urban ambient SFX.

### Food & beverage
- Defer to the **food-ad** skill for full hero-take detail (smash, cheese pull,
  sear, pour, bite) — it's more specialized than this generalist path. If used here
  anyway: macro on texture, steam/heat effects, warm chiaroscuro lighting, diegetic
  SFX only (sizzle, pour, crunch), no music.

### Tech / gadget
- **Shots:** clean tabletop or minimal-set hero shots, slow orbit or turntable
  move, macro on materials/ports/texture, a hands-on demonstration insert.
- **Camera:** macro 100mm for texture and detail, smooth slow dolly/orbit, precision
  push-ins — everything reads controlled and deliberate, never chaotic.
- **Lighting:** clean high-key softbox lighting with subtle rim light to separate
  the product from the background; minimal color, let the product's own material
  read.
- **Drone:** no.
- **SFX:** soft mechanical clicks, UI/interface tones (subtle, not musical), fabric
  or material texture sounds on touch.
- **Variations:** premium/flagship → slower, more negative space, near-silent SFX.
  Consumer/mass-market → slightly faster pacing, more human hands-on interaction.

### Real estate / architecture
- **Shots:** drone establishing flyover/reveal, wide interior dolly through rooms,
  detail inserts on materials/light fixtures/views, a slow reveal of the best room
  or view last.
- **Camera:** wide for interiors, drone for exteriors/aerials, smooth gimbal moves
  throughout — steady is the brand code here (nothing frantic).
- **Lighting:** golden hour exteriors, natural window light interiors, warm
  practical lighting for evening/lifestyle shots.
- **Drone:** yes — this segment leans on it more than most.
- **SFX:** ambient room tone, wind (exterior), footsteps, door/window sounds.

### Beauty / cosmetics
- **Shots:** extreme macro on skin/product texture, slow-motion product
  application or droplet/swatch moment, soft close-up on the person's face/features.
- **Camera:** 100mm macro dominant, slow motion (0.3x-0.5x) on the hero application
  moment, minimal camera movement otherwise — let the texture do the work.
- **Lighting:** soft diffused key light, no hard shadows, gentle rim light to
  outline skin/product; clean, almost clinical brightness for premium/derma brands,
  warmer glow for indulgent/luxury brands.
- **Drone:** no.
- **SFX:** soft product sounds (cap opening, texture/cream sounds), minimal ambient.

### Fitness / sports
- Defer to the **workout-hype** skill for full detail (it's built specifically for
  training/gym energy). If used here for a broader sports-brand film: fast cuts
  rotating close-up/medium/wide, high energy, hero-rep or peak-action moment as the
  climax.

### Corporate / institutional
- **Shots:** steady locked-off or gentle handheld shots of people at work,
  environmental establishing shots of the space, close-ups on hands/faces conveying
  competence and trust.
- **Camera:** minimal movement — a locked frame or a slow, barely-there push-in;
  nothing flashy, since the brand code here is trustworthiness, not spectacle.
- **Lighting:** natural window light or clean, even practical lighting; avoid heavy
  chiaroscuro (reads as too dramatic for this segment).
- **Drone:** only if the brief involves a campus/facility exterior.
- **SFX:** ambient office/workspace tone, quiet keyboard/footstep/door sounds.

### Travel / hospitality
- **Shots:** sweeping drone establishing shots of the destination, a POV or
  tracking shot through a space (lobby, trail, street), a quiet human moment
  (a look, a toast, a view) as the emotional beat.
- **Camera:** wide for scale, drone for scale/scope, slow dolly/tracking for
  immersion — pacing is unhurried, this segment sells time and space.
- **Lighting:** golden hour is the segment's signature; blue hour for a luxury/
  nightlife angle.
- **Drone:** yes — often the segment's hook shot.
- **SFX:** ambient environment (waves, wind, city, birdsong), footsteps, a soft
  human moment sound (laughter, glass clink) — never music.

## Universal camera vocabulary

- **Scale/action:** 24mm wide, low-angle tracking, drone flyover/reveal.
- **People/product intro:** 50-85mm medium, slow dolly, gentle push-in.
- **Texture/detail:** 100mm macro, slow orbit or rack focus.
- **Climax/reveal:** 360° orbit, bullet-time pause, hero push-in, motion-blur
  pull-out, match cut into the closing brand frame.

## Universal lighting vocabulary

- **Chiaroscuro:** hard directional key + rim light + deep shadow — premium,
  dramatic (automotive, luxury goods).
- **High-key soft:** even, shadow-light, bright — clean, trustworthy, modern (tech,
  beauty, corporate).
- **Golden hour / practical warmth:** low warm sun or warm practicals — human,
  aspirational (travel, real estate, lifestyle apparel).
- **Available/hard light:** less controlled, more raw — youth, streetwear,
  authenticity-coded brands.

## Universal transition vocabulary

Never leave a transition generic. Name it: **hard cut** (only when intentional
rhythm calls for it), **match cut** (shape/motion continuity between beats), **whip
pan**, **speed ramp**, **morph**, **smash cut** (for a hook's shock beat).

## SFX cheat sheet (diegetic only, no music)

- **Impact/action:** footsteps, fabric, door/latch, engine, tire, wind.
- **Detail/texture:** material sounds — creak, click, pour, sizzle, cap-open.
- **Ambient bed:** room tone, environment (traffic, nature, crowd) kept low under
  the beat sounds, never as a musical score.
- **Transitions:** a soft whoosh on a whip-pan (still diegetic-flavored, not a
  musical sting).

## How to add a new segment

When you build conventions for a segment not listed here and the user is happy with
the result, append a new entry in this format so the library grows:

```
### <Segment name>
- **Shots:** <typical shot types for this segment>
- **Camera:** <lens choices, movement style>
- **Lighting:** <the segment's lighting signature>
- **Drone:** <yes/no/only-when — and why>
- **SFX:** <typical diegetic palette>
- **Variations:** <how sub-styles within the segment change the above>
```
